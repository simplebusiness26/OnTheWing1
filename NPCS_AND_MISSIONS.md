# NPCS AND MISSIONS — ON THE WING

This file gives the coding model enough content to populate the first prison wing and scaffold a full 30-mission campaign. Names, dialogue and balance can be improved later, but these characters should already produce distinct schedules, relationships and story hooks.

All characters are fictional adults. The prison and procedures are fictionalized. Any illicit/escape-related content must stay abstract and must not become real-world instructional material.

---

# 1. IMPORTANT INMATES

## 1. Malcolm “Mal” Reed

Role: older respected games-table regular.

Personality:

- observant
- dry humor
- patient
- does not like being rushed
- remembers promises

Social position:

- respected without being feared
- friendly with older inmates
- low officer attention

Routine:

- morning: slow start, wing association
- work period: library/education placeholder route
- afternoon: often game table
- evening: checkers/chess

Gameplay hook:

Malcolm is one of the first people who teaches the player that conversation access is earned through time and behavior. He prefers to talk over games. Beating him at checkers raises Respect slightly; behaving badly after winning reduces warmth.

Story value:

- mentor/information route
- possible calm adviser in later acts
- can vouch socially for a trustworthy player

Heat sensitivity: high. If player becomes red hot, Malcolm temporarily distances himself.

Combat: weak/avoids fights.

# 2. OFFICERS

Officers are characters, not generic police AI. They have individual dispositions and memories but share abstract staff information through the Heat/Intel system.

## Officer Sarah Haines

Role: professional, fair, observant.

Personality:

- calm
- consistent
- rewards reliability
- does not overreact to one mistake

Gameplay:

Good relationship develops through routine/work, not flattery.

Can provide clearer warnings before sanctions if Staff Trust is high.

---

## Officer Mark “Millsy” Mills

Role: dry humor, knows the wing personalities.

Personality:

- sarcastic
- sharp observer
- dislikes obvious nonsense

Gameplay:

Often supplies humor. Becomes noticeably less friendly as Heat rises.

---

## Officer Patel

Role: rules-focused officer.

Personality:

- precise
- less interested in personal excuses

Gameplay:

Strongly affected by Compliance/Reliability. Useful for teaching routine consequences.

---

## Officer Graham Pike

Role: suspicious veteran officer.

Personality:

- wary
- notices association patterns quickly
- difficult to win over

Gameplay:

Higher baseline suspicion toward Veteran/Fighter archetypes. Should never become a cartoon villain.

---

## Senior Officer Rachel Ward

Role: supervisory presence.

Personality:

- measured
- rarely seen in everyday banter
- appears when patterns become serious

Gameplay:

Handles job reviews, privilege outcomes and some major story consequences. Her appearance itself signals that the player’s staff-facing state has become important.

# 4. MAIN CAMPAIGN — 30 MISSIONS

The following is a complete high-level campaign scaffold. The first three should be fully playable first. Later missions can begin as data definitions and become progressively authored.

## ACT I — FRESH RECEPTION

### Mission 01 — First Morning

Goal: teach movement and routine through play.

Beats:

- Wake in player cell.
- Morning unlock.
- Exit to landing.
- Learn camera/movement.
- Speak to Si or Malcolm depending spawn route.
- Reach morning routine zone within grace period.
- Officer reacts differently if player is late.

Systems introduced:

- movement
- camera
- interaction
- game clock
- routine
- officer response

Completion reward:

- Journal unlocked.
- small Prison Knowledge XP.

---

### Mission 02 — Find Your Feet

Goal: establish life-sim basics.

Flexible objectives:

- Shower.
- Try one training station.
- Speak to three inmates.
- Meet Officer Haines or Patel.
- Accept first job opportunity.

Systems:

- Hygiene
- training
- stats
- dialogue
- jobs

Completion:

- job assignment available.

---

### Mission 03 — Who You Sit With

Goal: teach social consequences.

Beats:

- Reece’s gym crowd invites player to join them during association.
- Malcolm/Oz group offers a game-table alternative.
- Player may choose one, split time, or ignore both.
- Nearby NPCs/officer witness association.

Consequences:

- different relationship memories.
- small group reputation effects.
- different side missions available.

---

### Mission 04 — Earn Your Keep

Goal: first proper work shift.

Beats:

- Report to Si.
- Complete wing-orderly tasks.
- Vince appears with a tempting unrelated social opportunity during work window.
- Player chooses whether to stay reliable or leave.

Consequences:

- Staff Trust/Reliability versus Vince relationship.

---

### Mission 05 — The Game

Goal: make minigame socially meaningful.

Beats:

- Malcolm agrees to discuss the wing only after a match.
- Play checkers.
- Winning/losing changes dialogue but both routes continue.
- Malcolm points player toward several key personalities.

Systems:

- minigame
- memory
- relationship-dependent dialogue

---

### Mission 06 — Lines on the Wing

Goal: reveal the existing power structure.

Beats:

- Witness dispute between Cal and one gym inmate.
- Player can intervene socially, support someone, or walk away.
- Vic is seen resolving another separate issue without force.
- Daz explains that influence works differently from strength.

Act-end state:

- player now understands multiple forms of power.

---

# ACT II — FINDING YOUR PLACE

### Mission 07 — Build Yourself

Goal: require meaningful progression.

Beats:

- Player chooses a target skill area.
- Complete several training/work/social progress actions across at least two in-game days.
- Reece/Cal/Oz reacts depending build.

Completion condition:

- raise any one core skill by configured amount and maintain routine sufficiently.

---

### Mission 08 — A Favor Owed

Goal: introduce favor/debt system.

Beats:

- Vince asks for a harmless/abstract favor represented by talking to another NPC and carrying a generic story message/token.
- Player can agree, refuse or renegotiate.
- Keeping commitment creates strong memory.

No realistic illegal instructions.

---

### Mission 09 — Red Hot

Goal: teach Heat through lived consequences.

Trigger:

- either natural player Heat reaches threshold or scripted public incident creates temporary Heat.

Beats:

- Nate refuses conversation.
- Officer Mills changes tone.
- Malcolm advises player to keep their head down.
- Player must spend a period avoiding further trouble and attending routine.

Completion:

- Heat drops below threshold.

---

### Mission 10 — The Challenge

Goal: combat progression check.

Beats:

- Cal or another medium fighter challenges player after prior interactions.
- Player may fight, defer, talk out of it with enough social stats, or arrange a later challenge.
- Fight is non-graphic.

Consequences:

- Respect/Fear/Heat/memory.

---

### Mission 11 — Quiet People

Goal: introduce Nate and low-profile network.

Prerequisite:

- Heat below threshold.

Beats:

- Ellis introduces player to Nate if relationship is sufficient, otherwise alternative route through work.
- Nate tests reliability through a simple promise/time meeting.

Completion:

- Nate becomes a known relationship.

---

### Mission 12 — Get in the Room

Goal: gain access to Vic socially.

Possible routes:

- high Respect.
- Malcolm introduction.
- Vince introduction with favor debt.
- Ellis/Nate trust route.
- Reece route if physical reputation is strong.

Beats:

- first real conversation with Vic.
- Vic asks what the player actually wants from prison life.

Act-end state:

- major long-term story flag set based on answer.

---

# ACT III — SOMETHING BIGGER

### Mission 13 — The Idea

Goal: introduce the central long-term fictional plan.

Beats:

- Daz/Vic/Vince route reveals that several inmates are considering a risky future possibility.
- The plan remains intentionally abstract.
- Player can show interest, skepticism or reject it for now.

Branch:

- risky-plan route flag.
- legitimate route remains available.

---

### Mission 14 — People, Not Pieces

Goal: force ally selection.

Beats:

- Player needs two trustworthy people for different roles in the abstract plan.
- Candidate relationships depend on side stories.
- Choosing one can upset another.

No physical escape method is specified.

---

### Mission 15 — The Right Reputation

Goal: make reputation a gating system.

Requirement:

- reach configured combination of Respect/Trust/Influence OR a social alternative.

Gameplay:

- resolve a wing dispute, complete work or help several people depending build.

---

### Mission 16 — An Ordinary Day

Goal: dramatic contrast and simulation test.

The game asks the player to keep routine for one full in-game day while several optional temptations/events occur.

Success:

- lowers Heat.
- raises reliability.
- unlocks a quiet meeting.

---

### Mission 17 — The Coded Note

Goal: add an abstract story item.

Beats:

- Obtain a fictional coded-note token through one of several social routes.
- The item itself contains no real operational information.
- Thinker/Awareness characters may understand an optional story clue.

---

### Mission 18 — Pick a Side

Goal: first major irreversible relationship split.

Conflict:

- Vince and Nate disagree over who can be trusted.
- Player can support one, mediate with Oz, or attempt neutrality with high Influence.

Act-end:

- sets loyalty branch for Act IV.

---

# ACT IV — PRESSURE

### Mission 19 — Eyes on You

Goal: staff network becomes active.

Beats:

- Officer Pike’s suspicion rises based on previous state.
- Some NPCs distance themselves.
- Player must choose whether to continue risky story branch or temporarily prioritize routine.

---

### Mission 20 — Cracks

Goal: expose ally reliability.

Beats:

- one selected ally fails to show, panics or demands something depending hidden reliability.
- prior Trust memories influence whether they recover.

No real-world operational detail.

---

### Mission 21 — The Job Review

Goal: connect staff and story systems.

Trigger variants:

- low reliability → formal review.
- high reliability → promotion/alternative work access.

Senior Officer Ward becomes important.

Outcome changes later routes.

---

### Mission 22 — Settle It

Goal: status conflict.

Reece/Cal branch or another rival forces a confrontation.

Options:

- fight.
- social settlement.
- mediation.
- call in favor.

Results should reflect stats and relationships accumulated across game.

---

### Mission 23 — Someone Talks

Goal: betrayal tension without teaching concealment/evasion.

Beats:

- Staff appear to know more than expected.
- Player investigates socially who broke trust.
- Several suspects exist.
- Memory system provides clues.

The truth varies based on selected allies/earlier choices where feasible.

---

### Mission 24 — Lockdown

Goal: high-pressure act finale.

A fictional wing-wide security incident causes temporary restricted routine.

Gameplay:

- relationships play out through cell/landing conversations.
- the player cannot simply roam everywhere.
- allies react differently under pressure.

No realistic security procedures or bypass instructions.

Act-end:

- one major ally relationship changes permanently.

---

# ACT V — THE ENDGAME

### Mission 25 — What Are You Doing This For?

Goal: clarify player’s endgame based on history.

Conversation with Malcolm/Oz/Ellis/Vic depending route.

Possible commitments:

- continue risky fictional escape route.
- pursue legitimate release route.
- accept/seek transfer route.
- pursue influence/status route.

This is not the sole ending decision; it weights existing state.

---

### Mission 26 — Call in the Favors

Goal: use relationships accumulated across entire game.

Beats:

- ask 2–4 NPCs for support.
- results depend on memories/debts.
- Marty can unexpectedly help if early side story was completed.

---

### Mission 27 — Too Much Heat

Goal: final Heat management test.

High-Heat route:

- risky opportunities close.
- player needs a different ending route or must regain stability over several days.

Low-Heat route:

- more options remain.

No evasion mechanics.

---

### Mission 28 — The Choice of Trust

Goal: final ally decision.

Beats:

- two key people cannot both be relied upon for the same role/story outcome.
- player chooses based on accumulated knowledge.
- hidden reliability + memories determine whether trust pays off.

---

### Mission 29 — Last Night on the Wing

Goal: emotional payoff.

Beats:

- evening free-roam with unique dialogue from major NPCs.
- relationships acknowledge long-term history.
- optional final games/training/work goodbye moments.
- save locks in final world state for ending calculation.

---

### Mission 30 — The Way Out

Goal: resolve campaign.

Ending selection reads:

- player commitment.
- Heat.
- Staff Trust/Reliability.
- Respect/Trust/Fear/Influence.
- key NPC relationships.
- side-mission flags.
- betrayal status.
- selected character.

Possible outcomes:

- abstract fictional escape ending.
- legitimate release ending.
- transfer ending.
- influence ending.
- betrayal/collapse ending.

If escape route is selected, present it as a story/cinematic outcome without realistic escape instructions.

# 7. DIALOGUE VOICE GUIDANCE

Characters should sound distinct without becoming caricatures.

Guidelines:

- UK conversational English.
- Use slang naturally but sparingly.
- Avoid every sentence being packed with prison slang.
- Officers call out routine clearly and professionally.
- Humor comes from personality.
- Do not make every prisoner aggressive.
- Important NPCs should occasionally say nothing useful; not every conversation is a quest.

Examples of tone, not final required lines:

Malcolm: understated, patient, slightly amused.

Reece: short direct sentences.

Vince: persuasive, always framing things as mutually beneficial.

Tobes: energetic, absurd confidence.

Ellis: practical, low drama.

Oz: thoughtful and dry.

Deano: loud, impulsive.

Vic: quiet, economical language.

Haines: fair and clear.

Mills: dry sarcasm.

# 9. LATE-GAME MEMORY CALLBACKS

Mission 29 should query important memories and generate at least 3–5 personalized conversations.

Examples:

- Malcolm remembers first game result.
- Si remembers work streak or repeated lateness.
- Cal remembers whether player humiliated or encouraged him.
- Tobes remembers whether player listened to his nonsense when nobody else would.
- Marty remembers the early side mission.
- Nate remembers whether player respected his request for distance during high Heat.

This is a core payoff for the memory system and should be treated as important content, not optional polish.
